<b>Note:</b> This is a VR experiment, so idea is to play with the variables. Suggested steps, to utilize the 
simulation and learn from the changes, are: <br>

1. Select a microstructure and from its scale bar, try to get a rough estimate on the grain size of 
the sample. This will allow you to get a feel of how close the grain size analysis method 
provide the result. <br>

2. Measure the grain size using ASTM method. Herein, get the microstructure, and then obtain the number of grains at 100× magnification in 1 in<sup>2</sup> area. First, obtain the ASTM grain size (n) 
using the equation: <br>
N=2<sup>n-1</sup> ,<br>
where <b>N</b> is the number of grains at 100x in 1 in<sup>2</sup> area. <br>

Then, change the magnification (to start with, say 50×, or 200×). Accordingly, at 200×, one will obtain one-fourth (1/4<sup>th</sup>) number of the grains as the magnification has increased (both in x and y direction). Then check how the values change. <br><br>

3. For Jeffries method, utilize a circle with an area of 5000 mm<sup>2</sup> and count the number  of grains. Now, obtain the number of grains per mm<sup>2</sup> (N<sub>A</sub>) as per the following equation: <br>

N<sub>𝐴</sub>=𝑀<sup>2</sup>/5000 x (𝑛<sub>1</sub>+𝑛<sub>2</sub>/2) <br>
where n<sub>1</sub> is the number of full grains, n<sub>2</sub> is the number of grains intersected at circumference, and M is the magnification. The value of 5000 in the denominator corresponds to area of 5000 mm<sup>2</sup> (of circle) where the grains are captured. <br>

Now average grain area (Ā), with units of mm<sup>2</sup>, is obtained as 1/N<sub>A</sub>, and an average 
grain area in µm<sup>2</sup> is given as : <br>

Ā (µm<sup>2</sup>) = 10<sup>6</sup>/N<sub>A</sub> <br>

Correspondingly, the mean diameter (d) can be given as: <br>
d̄(𝜇𝑚)= (𝐴̅)<sup>1/2</sup> = 10<sup>3</sup>/N<sub>A</sub><sup>1/2</sup> <br><br>

4. In the grain boundary triple points (P) method, the triple points in an area are counted with quadruple points (where four grains meet) are accounted a weight of ‘two’. Accordingly, the number of grains per unit area (N<sub>A</sub>) is calculated using the equation: <br>

N<sub>A</sub> = (𝑃/2 + 1) / A<sub>T</sub> <br>
 
where A<sub>T</sub> is the area at 1× magnification, and the corresponding ASTM grain size, (n) is obtained as: <br>
n = 3.322log ((𝑃/2 + 1) / A<sub>T</sub>) - 2.95 <br><br>
